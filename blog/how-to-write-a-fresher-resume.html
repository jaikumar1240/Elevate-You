How to Write a Fresher Resume That Gets Shortlisted

By Jai Rajput · Updated 5 November 2026 · 7 min read

Recruiters spend only a few seconds on a first scan of your resume. If you are a student or fresher with no work experience, the goal is simple: make your strongest evidence of skill obvious within that scan. Here is how to do it.

1. Keep it to one page

With limited experience, a second page almost always means padding. One clean page forces you to keep only what proves you can do the job. Use a single column, a standard font (Calibri, Arial or similar) at 10–11 pt, and consistent spacing. Fancy templates with icons, skill bars and photos often break applicant tracking systems (ATS), so the parser may read your resume as a jumble.

2. Use this section order

  1. Header: name, phone, professional email, city, LinkedIn and GitHub (or portfolio) links.
  2. Summary (optional, 2 lines): your target role and your strongest skills.
  3. Education: degree, college, graduation year, CGPA if it is strong.
  4. Projects: your most important section if you have no experience.
  5. Internships / experience: including freelance and club work.
  6. Skills: grouped by type (languages, tools, frameworks).
  7. Achievements and certifications: only relevant ones.

If you have a good internship, put it above Projects. Otherwise lead with Projects right after Education.

3. Write projects like results, not tasks

Most fresher resumes say "Developed a website using HTML, CSS and JavaScript." That tells the recruiter nothing about your ability. Use this formula for each bullet: action verb + what you built + how + outcome.

Weak: Made a food ordering app using React.
Better: Built a food ordering web app in React and Firebase with live cart updates and login; used by 40+ classmates during a hostel pilot.

If you cannot measure an outcome, describe scale or complexity: number of users, pages, records handled, APIs integrated, or the problem it solved. Never invent numbers; interviewers will ask about them.

4. Tailor your skills section to the job

Read the job description and mirror its keywords where they truthfully apply to you. ATS filters and recruiters both search for specific terms. List only skills you could discuss in an interview. A claim like "Python: Advanced" invites hard questions, so be honest with your level.

5. Use strong, specific action verbs

Start bullets with verbs like built, designed, automated, analysed, reduced, led, organised, presented. Avoid "responsible for", "worked on" and "helped with"; they hide what you personally did.

6. Resume mistakes that cost freshers interviews

  • Typos and inconsistent date formats.
  • An unprofessional email address.
  • A long objective statement such as "seeking a challenging position…".
  • Listing every school-level achievement while hiding real projects.
  • Broken or private GitHub and LinkedIn links.
  • Sending one identical resume to every role.

For more detail, read our guide on resume mistakes freshers make.

7. Final checklist before you apply

  • Save as PDF named YourName-Role.pdf.
  • Open the PDF and try to select the text. If you cannot, ATS cannot read it either.
  • Ask someone to read it for 10 seconds, then ask what you do. If they cannot tell, rewrite your top third.
