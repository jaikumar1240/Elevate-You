Common HR Interview Questions for Freshers (with Sample Answers)

By Jai Rajput · Updated 5 November 2026 · 8 min read

HR rounds look easy, yet many freshers get rejected here. The reason is rarely the question; it is rambling, generic answers. Below are the questions you will almost certainly face, and a simple structure for each.

1. Tell me about yourself

This is not a request for your life story. Use present → past → future in 60–90 seconds.

"I'm a final-year B.Tech student in computer science, focused on web development. Over the last year I built two projects, including an expense tracker used by my hostel, and completed a three-month internship where I worked on APIs. I'm now looking for a role where I can grow as a backend developer, which is why this position caught my eye."

Do not recite your resume line by line, and do not start with your school marks.

2. What are your strengths?

Choose two strengths relevant to the role and prove each with a short example. "I learn fast" is weak. "I taught myself React in three weeks to ship my college fest website" is believable.

3. What is your weakness?

Pick a real but non-critical weakness, then show the fix. For example: "I used to hesitate to speak in group discussions, so I joined a college debate club and now volunteer to present in team meetings." Avoid "I'm a perfectionist" and avoid weaknesses essential to the job.

4. Why do you want to join our company?

Research before the interview: what the company builds, its customers, recent news. Then connect it to your goals: "Your team works on payments infrastructure at scale, which is exactly the kind of problem I want to learn from." A generic answer such as "it's a reputed company" shows no preparation.

5. Why should we hire you?

Match two or three requirements from the job description to proof from your projects or internships. End with your attitude: you learn quickly, take feedback well and will be productive early.

6. Where do you see yourself in five years?

Be honest but grounded: you want to build strong fundamentals, take ownership of larger modules and grow towards more responsibility. Do not say you want the interviewer's job, and do not say you are unsure.

7. Are you willing to relocate or work in shifts?

Answer truthfully. If you have constraints, say so politely and early. If you are flexible, say so with confidence.

8. Do you have any questions for us?

Always ask something. Good examples: "What does success look like in the first six months?", "How is feedback given to new joiners?", "What does the training programme cover?" Avoid asking about salary or leave in a first round.

How to practise

  • Write each answer in bullet points, not full sentences, so you do not sound scripted.
  • Record yourself on your phone and check for filler words and pace.
  • Practise with another person who will interrupt and ask follow-up questions.
