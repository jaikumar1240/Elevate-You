Why You Keep Getting Rejected in Interviews (and How to Fix It)

By Jai Rajput · Updated 5 November 2026 · 6 min read

If you are reaching interviews but never getting offers, the problem is almost never "bad luck". It is usually one or two specific, fixable blockers that nobody has told you about, because companies rarely give feedback. Here are the most common ones.

1. Your answers are too long and unstructured

Interviewers decide quickly whether you can communicate clearly. Rambling for three minutes buries your point. Use a simple structure: answer first, reason second, example third. Practise keeping most answers under 90 seconds.

2. You cannot explain your own projects

Interviewers pick a project and dig. If you cannot explain why you chose a technology, how a feature works or what went wrong, they assume the work was copied. Prepare a two-minute explanation for each project: the problem, your role, one technical decision, one challenge, and what you would improve.

3. You give generic answers to "Why this company?"

If your answer would fit any company, it shows no effort. Spend 20 minutes before each interview reading the company's website, products and recent news, and weave one specific detail into your answer.

4. You freeze on questions you do not know

Silence or "I don't know" ends the conversation. A better response: "I haven't worked with that directly, but here is how I would approach it…" and then reason aloud. Interviewers often judge your thinking more than the final answer.

5. Your fundamentals are shaky

Many freshers prepare advanced topics but stumble on basics such as OOP concepts, SQL joins, or core subject definitions. Keep a short list of fundamentals and revise it weekly.

6. Your resume invites the wrong questions

Listing skills you barely know turns the interview into a trap. Cut anything you cannot discuss confidently. See our fresher resume guide.

7. Body language and tone work against you

Avoid reading from notes, looking away constantly or speaking in a flat, rushed voice. For video interviews, check your camera angle, lighting and audio beforehand, and look at the camera when you speak.

8. You never get feedback, so you repeat the same mistakes

This is the biggest one. Without outside feedback, you practise your mistakes. After every interview write down the questions you struggled with. Better, do a mock interview with someone who gives honest, specific feedback.

A simple self-diagnosis

  • Rejected in the first screening? Look at your resume and applications.
  • Rejected after the technical round? Look at fundamentals and problem solving.
  • Rejected after the HR round? Look at communication, preparation and attitude.

Use this to focus your effort. A structured preparation plan helps you avoid trying to fix everything at once.
