LinkedIn Profile Tips for Freshers and Students

By Jai Rajput · Published 18 November 2026 · 7 min read

Recruiters in India regularly search LinkedIn for freshers, and many will look at your profile after reading your resume. You do not need years of experience to have a strong profile. You need a clear direction and proof of what you have built.

1. Write a headline that shows direction

Your headline appears next to your name in search results, so “Student at XYZ College” wastes it. LinkedIn’s own advice for students is to pair what you already do with the role or field you want next.

  • Final-year B.Tech CSE | Java, Spring Boot, SQL | Looking for backend developer roles
  • B.Com graduate | Excel, Tally, GST | Aspiring financial analyst
  • BA English | Content writing and social media | Open to content marketing roles

Include the keywords a recruiter would type when searching for someone like you.

2. Add a clear, recent photo

A photo helps people recognise you and makes them more comfortable connecting. It does not need to be a studio headshot: a well-lit, recent photo of your face against a plain background is enough. Add a simple banner image related to your field if you can.

3. Use the About section to tell a short story

Keep it to one or two short paragraphs. A simple template:

I’m a [degree] student at [college] focused on [field]. I’ve built [one or two projects] using [skills], and during [internship or activity] I [specific contribution]. I’m currently looking for [role type] where I can [what you want to do]. Feel free to reach out at [professional email].

4. Show projects and internships with impact

Add internships, freelance work, club leadership and volunteering to the Experience section. Use the Projects section for academic and personal projects, with a link to GitHub, a live demo or a document. Describe what you improved, created or supported, not just what you were responsible for, exactly as you would on a fresher resume.

5. Add relevant skills and certifications

List skills that match the jobs you want and that you could discuss in an interview. Put your strongest skills at the top. Add certifications from recognised platforms only if they are relevant to your target role.

6. Tell recruiters you are open to work

Use the Open to Work setting and choose your job titles, locations and whether you want on-site, hybrid or remote roles. You can show it only to recruiters if you prefer. Also set a custom public profile URL (for example linkedin.com/in/yourname) and use it on your resume.

7. Be active, even a little

  • Connect with classmates, seniors, professors and people you meet at events, with a short personal note.
  • Follow companies you want to join and turn on job alerts.
  • Post when you finish a project, internship or certification: what you built, what you learned and a link.
  • Comment thoughtfully on posts in your field instead of only liking them.

Quick checklist

  • Headline includes your target role and two or three key skills.
  • Clear photo and a custom profile URL.
  • About section of one or two short paragraphs.
  • At least two projects with links.
  • Skills match the jobs you are applying for.
  • Open to Work preferences are set.
