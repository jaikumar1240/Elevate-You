Tell Me About Yourself: Best Answer for Freshers With Examples

By Jai Rajput · Published 18 November 2026 · 6 min read

“Tell me about yourself” is usually the first interview question, and it sets the tone for everything that follows. Freshers often either recite their resume line by line or freeze. Here is a simple structure you can prepare once and adapt to any role.

What the interviewer is really asking

The interviewer is not asking for your life story. They want a short, confident summary that answers three things: who you are professionally, what you can already do, and why you are interested in this role. It is also a test of communication: can you organise your thoughts and speak clearly for about a minute?

Use the present, past, future structure

Career services teams such as the University of Pennsylvania recommend a three-part story. For a fresher it looks like this:

  1. Present (1–2 sentences): your degree, college, and the area you are focused on.
  2. Past (2–3 sentences): one or two projects, internships or activities that built skills relevant to this job. Mention a concrete result if you have one.
  3. Future (1–2 sentences): why this role or company fits what you want to do next.

Aim for 45 to 90 seconds. Past two minutes, most interviewers start to lose interest.

Sample answer: engineering fresher

“I’m a final-year B.Tech Computer Science student at [College], and I’ve mostly focused on backend development. In my third year I built an attendance app in Node.js and Firebase that our department now uses for two sections, and during a two-month internship at [Company] I wrote APIs and fixed bugs in a live product, which taught me how code reviews and deadlines work in a real team. I’m applying for this software engineer role because your team works on large-scale APIs, and I want to get much better at building reliable systems.”

Sample answer: commerce or BBA fresher

“I recently completed my B.Com from [College], where I was most interested in financial accounting and analysis. I completed a six-week internship with a CA firm, where I reconciled GST returns for small businesses and learned to work carefully under deadline. I also led the finance team for our college fest, managing a budget of around ₹3 lakh. I’m excited about this analyst role because it combines accounting accuracy with the business decisions I enjoy learning about.”

Sample answer: arts or humanities fresher

“I’m a BA English graduate from [College], and writing has been my strongest skill. I ran our department’s newsletter for a year and grew its readership through Instagram, and I’ve freelanced short blog articles for two small brands. I’m applying for this content associate role because I want to learn how to write for a specific audience and measure what actually performs.”

Replace every bracket and detail with your own true experience. Interviewers will ask follow-up questions about anything you mention.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Reading out your resume. They already have it. Pick the two most relevant points.
  • Starting with personal details. Your hometown, parents’ jobs and hobbies are rarely relevant unless they connect to the role.
  • Generic adjectives. “I am hard-working and passionate” is forgettable. Show it with one example instead.
  • Memorising word for word. Remember the structure and key points so it sounds natural.
  • No link to the job. Always end with why this role makes sense for you.

How to practise

Write your answer in bullet points, not a script. Record yourself on your phone, time it, and listen back once. Then practise with a friend or mentor who will ask a follow-up question on whatever you mention. Prepare a short and a slightly longer version so you can adjust to the interviewer.
