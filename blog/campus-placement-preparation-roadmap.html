Campus Placement Preparation Roadmap: A 6-Month Plan for Students

By Jai Rajput · Updated 5 November 2026 · 8 min read

Most students start preparing when the first company arrives on campus, which is too late. Placements are won in the months before. Here is a realistic six-month plan you can adjust to your branch and target companies.

Before you start: know your target

Placement preparation differs by company type. Service companies lean on aptitude, basic coding and communication. Product companies and startups lean on data structures, problem solving and projects. Core-branch roles focus on subject fundamentals. Decide your top two target categories, then weigh your plan accordingly.

Month 1: Audit and foundations

  • List the companies visiting your campus last year, their eligibility criteria and their selection rounds.
  • Take one aptitude test and one basic coding test to find your starting level.
  • Create a first draft of your resume (see our fresher resume guide).
  • Set a daily routine: 90 minutes minimum, same time every day.

Month 2: Aptitude and communication

  • Quantitative aptitude: percentages, ratios, time and work, probability, number series.
  • Logical reasoning and verbal ability: puzzles, arrangements, reading comprehension.
  • Start speaking practice: explain one concept aloud for two minutes every day.

Month 3: Technical fundamentals

  • For software roles: one language well, arrays, strings, recursion, sorting and searching, basic SQL.
  • Revise core subjects: operating systems, DBMS, computer networks, OOP.
  • For core branches: revise your main subjects and project topics thoroughly.

Month 4: Projects and problem solving

  • Finish or polish two projects that you can explain end to end: why you built it, trade-offs, what you would improve.
  • Solve problems steadily on a coding platform, focusing on patterns rather than quantity.
  • Update your LinkedIn profile and GitHub README files.

Month 5: Interviews and mock tests

  • Prepare answers for common HR questions.
  • Do at least three full mock interviews with feedback from someone experienced.
  • Practise group discussions and one-minute self-introductions.
  • Take timed full-length aptitude and coding tests to build speed.

Month 6: Apply, refine, repeat

  • Apply to every eligible drive, including ones you consider "lower tier"; interview practice is valuable in itself.
  • After each round, write down what was asked and where you hesitated. Fix those gaps.
  • Research each company the day before: products, recent news, role details.
  • Keep sleep, health and routine steady. Burnout loses more offers than missing topics.

Common planning mistakes

  • Watching tutorials endlessly without practising.
  • Ignoring communication until the HR round.
  • Collecting certificates instead of building projects.
  • Comparing your progress with friends instead of your own plan.
