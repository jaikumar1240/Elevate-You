Why Should We Hire You: How Freshers Should Answer

By Jai Rajput · Published 18 November 2026 · 6 min read

“Why should we hire you?” feels uncomfortable when you have no full-time experience. But the question is not asking you to be better than everyone else. It asks you to connect what the job needs with evidence of what you can already do.

Why interviewers ask this question

Interviewers want to know three things: whether you understood what the role needs, whether you have proof that you can do some of it, and whether you can summarise your value clearly. Your answer also gives them a ready-made pitch to share with the rest of the hiring panel.

A three-step structure that works for freshers

  1. Find the top two or three needs. Read the job description. Underline the skills that appear first, most often, or in the title.
  2. Match each need to proof. For each one, pick a project, internship, competition, club role or course where you used that skill. Mention a result if you have one.
  3. Close with what you will bring. One sentence on how you will contribute or learn quickly in the first few months.

Keep it to about one minute. Specific beats modest, and specific beats boastful.

Sample answer: IT or software role

“The role needs someone comfortable with Java and SQL who can work in a team. In my final-year project, four of us built a library management system in Spring Boot and MySQL; I designed the database and wrote most of the search APIs. During my internship at [Company], I fixed around 20 bugs in a production codebase and learned to work through code reviews. I can contribute to your backend tasks from the start, and I learn new tools quickly, as I did when I picked up Docker for that internship in two weeks.”

Sample answer: sales or business development role

“This role is about speaking to many people every day and handling rejection. As sponsorship head for our college fest, I cold-called over 60 local businesses and closed eight sponsors worth about ₹1.5 lakh. I also worked part-time at a retail store, where I learned to understand what a customer actually needs before recommending anything. I’m comfortable with targets, and I’d bring that same persistence to your team.”

Sample answer: finance or accounting role

“You need someone accurate with numbers and Excel. During my internship at a CA firm, I prepared reconciliations for 15 small-business clients and built an Excel template that cut our monthly checking time noticeably. I’ve also cleared [relevant certification or exam]. I’m detail-oriented, I meet deadlines, and I’m keen to learn your reporting processes quickly.”

Use only examples and numbers that are true. If you cannot measure a result, describe the scale or the problem you solved instead.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Talking about what you want. “This job will be great for my career” answers a different question.
  • Comparing yourself to other candidates. You do not know them. Focus on your own evidence.
  • Only listing qualities. “I am hard-working, honest and a team player” without proof sounds rehearsed.
  • One answer for every company. Adjust the skills you highlight to each job description.

Related questions to prepare

The same preparation helps with “What can you bring to the company?”, “Why are you the right fit?” and “What makes you different?”. Note that “Why do you want to work here?” is different: it is about your reasons for choosing the company, so research the company before the interview.
